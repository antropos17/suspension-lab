# Vehicle model revision

## Career tires — 2026-11-21

Career builds use nominal outer radii of 305–425 mm and 13–19 inch rims. Each wheel grade sets the actual rim fixture, tread-node radius, bead anchors and reference chamber volume. When fitting wheels, the replacement ring inherits hub motion and inflation. Grounded fits adjust the assembly height by the radius difference. The stand-alone reference simulation retains a 520 mm tire for comparisons with its earlier suspension and solver regressions. Measurements for the stock and maximum career builds are in [STATUS.md](STATUS.md).

## Tire stability correction — 2026-11-04

The production solver now uses eight 960 Hz substeps with 8 velocity / 4 position iterations instead of four 480 Hz substeps with 16 / 8 iterations. The velocity-iteration budget per game tick remains 64; the smaller step resolves the stiff belt and pressure dynamics more accurately. Rings above 32 nodes use at least 16 substeps. `heightAt` is an optional deterministic simulation fixture for flat-road verification; gameplay still uses the existing trail profile.

The renderer now fills the polygon through contact-body centers plus the actual contact circles. The former polygon through averaged outer normals could bridge through terrain vertices by 1–2 mm even when all physical bodies remained clear. The corrected fill follows the existing collision representation, without projecting the shape onto terrain or changing material softness.

Baseline rough-road probes found up to 6.2 mm vertical tread penetration at 3.2 bar. With the corrected solver, all three high-pressure drive probes crossed 95 m with no measured vertical penetration; a front-drive normal-distance probe retained at least 4.2 mm clearance to the actual terrain segments. Flat-road body vertical acceleration RMS changed from 0.0987 / 0.1649 / 0.2739 to 0.0505 / 0.0889 / 0.1067 m/s^2 at 0.6 / 2.2 / 3.2 bar. These are paired gameplay measurements, not real-vehicle calibration. Increasing contact bead radius and adding a bulk damping trial did not provide enough benefit and were reverted.

New behavioral regressions cover sub-0.1 mm settled hub ripple, a ramped load/unload with sub-1 mm solver-step jumps and ride-height recovery, flat-road judder bounds, and all nine drive/pressure combinations over the rough section. They check both vertical clearance and independent Euclidean clearance to nearby terrain segments, physical chamber area, and the rendered center-polygon bridges. An initial stability test accidentally changed step size immediately before measuring settled ripple; it was corrected to settle and measure at one constant step, retaining its tolerance.

Browser whole-course checks: FWD at 3.2 bar finished in 30.6 simulated seconds, RWD at 0.6 bar in 29.9, and AWD at 2.2 bar in 29.0. No captured JavaScript errors. These representative browser runs supplement the deterministic regressions; arbitrary terrains, impacts and hardware are not qualified.

The implementation description below records the preceding integration. Its four-substep references are superseded by this section.

An additional extreme-pressure hill audit found FWD at 3.2 bar gained only 1.961 m in the first three seconds after stopping. Reducing the low-speed slip target alone only raised that to 1.990 m, so that trial was reverted. The front-engine chassis center of mass was moved from x=0.20 to x=0.30 m; supported axle fractions are now derived from its actual local center and the 2.5 m wheelbase, rather than remaining hardcoded. The nominal front/rear split is 62/38. This is a game tuning choice, not measured vehicle mass data. FWD restart gains became 2.992 m at 0.6 bar and 2.691 m at 3.2 bar; RWD and AWD extreme-pressure cases also passed. The existing hill regression now covers all nine drive/pressure combinations with the unchanged >2 m criterion. The final FWD/3.2 bar browser run finished in 30.2 simulated seconds, without captured JS errors.

## Current pneumatic integration — 2026-11-04

This section supersedes earlier rigid-tire descriptions below. `src/pneumatic-tire.js` now supplies the game's tire contact geometry: 32 independent circular tread elements on an elastic belt, radial/offset sidewall links and a rotating rigid rim. There is no rigid outer tire circle and no renderer-only ground projection. The small 0.245 m rim circle exists for bottoming; normal rolling contact is carried by the tread bodies. The old `src/wheel-shape.js` was removed.

The nominal tire radius is 0.48 m, bead radius 0.048 m and centerline radius 0.432 m; carcass mass is 24 kg and effective width is 0.18 m. Chamber volume is width times signed tread polygon area minus rim disk area. At constant temperature, absolute pressure is `(101325 + inflationBar*100000)*referenceVolume/currentVolume`. Atmospheric pressure is subtracted before pressure forces are applied. A numerical floor of 15% reference chamber volume bounds extreme compression forces; this is not validated recovery from an inverted carcass. The inflation setting controls gas quantity at reference volume, while the UI separately displays actual rear/front operating pressures. Expansion means the operating value can be below the nominal setting.

Each counterclockwise polygon vertex receives `gaugePressure*width/2*(y_next-y_prev, x_prev-x_next)`, the area gradient. These internal forces sum to zero force and torque; sidewalls transfer pressure loads to the rim. Circumferential prestress balances nominal 2.2 bar inflation, and belt stiffness scales with segment length/effective mass. Offset links transmit drive and brake torque; a radial-only network cannot resist shear correctly. The material parameters are an original simplified elastic network, not measured rubber properties.

Each suspension carrier drives its rim through a revolute motor. The carrier's prismatic guide transmits reaction torque to the chassis. Terrain/liner contacts act on the tread; main chassis fixtures use a separate category so the tire does not lock against its own body. A slip-limited motor target uses rolling speed plus 5 rad/s, capped at 22 rad/s, to reduce excessive standstill wheelspin. Existing drive modes, braking torque and suspension forces remain. Four solver substeps per 120 Hz tick give 480 Hz; rings above 32 nodes receive at least eight substeps.

Rejected attempts: initial tread/chassis self-collision locked rotation and was corrected with filtering. Immediate 22 rad/s FWD spin narrowly failed a hill restart and was corrected with the slip-limited target. A 48-node ring at 1/480 s expanded incorrectly; increasing iterations alone did not fix it. At 1/960 s, 24/32/48-node bench hub heights were about 0.46829 / 0.46822 / 0.46855 m. Refinement therefore requires the smaller step. Old load tests applied force once before a game tick, which Planck cleared after its first substep; they now apply load*STEP impulses so the average applied load remains 1000 N rather than dropping when substeps increase. The suspension travel assertion was retained.

At 250 kg hub load, the 32-node bench settled near 0.4483 m hub height at nominal 0.6 bar (operating 0.7215 bar, active tread span 0.2615 m), and 0.4681 m at nominal 2.2 bar (operating 2.2109 bar, span 0.096 m). At 350 kg and 0.6 bar, height was about 0.4384 m and pressure 0.7262 bar. Span is a coarse diagnostic including bead diameter, not a calibrated continuous pressure footprint. Tests check inflation response, physical deformation, settling, small-rim clearance, node-derived outline, momentum balance and step/mesh refinement. Full-course clearance uses every node and bead radius. Contact-pair count now has a 1200 bound because each wheel has 32 contact bodies; zero geometric overlap and travel-limit assertions remain.

Browser evidence includes 0.6 bar rough-track driving and 260 m completion at 38.0 simulated seconds including idle/coasting, live inflation/restart retention and 3.2 bar driving, with no captured JS errors during the low-pressure run. Final command outcomes are recorded in STATUS.md.

Limits: this is a physically deforming two-dimensional pneumatic model, not calibrated 3D tire FEA. Temperature, lateral width deformation, wear, punctures and material damage are absent. Contact beads create discretization and a slightly scalloped surface. CPU cost is higher than rigid circles; mobile hardware and engineering accuracy are not qualified. The historical skill-install bench remains a separate earlier experiment.

Primary model references: [BeamNG pressure-wheel construction](https://documentation.beamng.com/modding/vehicle/sections/wheels/), [Chrono tire classes](https://api.chrono.projectchrono.org/wheeled_tire.html), [XPBD](https://mmacklin.com/xpbd.pdf), installed Planck DistanceJoint/RevoluteJoint APIs, and [OptimumG damping discussion](https://optimumg.com/spring-brake/). No external simulator or new dependency was installed.

## Sources and interpretation

- [MathWorks suspension comparison](https://de.mathworks.com/help/sdl/ug/suspension-system-comparison.html): sprung and unsprung masses, nonlinear spring/damper models. Applied as separate chassis, hub and tire bodies.
- [MathWorks hard-stop documentation](https://www.mathworks.com/help/releases/r2025a/pdf_doc/sdl/sdl_ref.pdf): smoothly transitioning stiffness/damping near full compression. Applied as progressive cubic bump and droop buffers before mechanical limits.
- [Nonlinear shock absorber research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.17175): digressive and progressive damping characteristics. Applied as an asymmetric, force-limited damping approximation, not an implementation of the paper's hydraulic model.
- [Box2D wheel joints](https://box2d.org/documentation/group__wheel__joint.html): wheel rotation, spring and travel constraints. The installed Planck version has different APIs; checked local implementation. Its WheelJoint.getJointSpeed returns angular speed, so translational damping uses PrismaticJoint.getJointSpeed instead.
- [Project Chrono tires](https://api.chrono.projectchrono.org/wheeled_tire.html): distinguishes rigid contact, handling and finite element tire models. This game uses spring compliance with rigid circular contacts, not FEA or a calibrated tire material.
- [SAE transient diesel emissions experiment](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/experimental-assessment-turbocharged-diesel-engine-transient-emissions-acceleration-load-change-starting-2010-01-1287): acceleration/load transients affect smoke. Only the public abstract was used. The exhaust artwork represents an older diesel; no emissions concentrations or calibration are inferred.

## Suspension

One prismatic guide per hub constrains alignment and rotation. Travel is limited from -0.25 to +0.24 m; segmented liners remain a last contact surface. Forces are applied before each 240 Hz solver substep.

For supported axle mass m, omega = 2*pi*f, k = m*omega^2. Spring force includes a static load preload and targets 0.045 m of compression. The damping coefficient is 2*zeta*m*omega, multiplied by 0.65 for compression and 1.2 for rebound. A tanh blow-off curve bounds damper force at 3000 N. Cubic buffers start at +0.14 and -0.14 m, progressively opposing further travel. Equal/opposite forces act on hub and chassis, preserving reaction forces and pitch.

Tire spring frequency is 8.5 Hz with 0.85 damping. Smaller visual rims are rigid and follow the physical hub independently of the outer tire; rubber shading and modest compression are drawn separately. Contact geometry remains circular: the flattened contact patch and carcass strain are not physically resolved.

## Exhaust

Emission rate responds to smoothed accelerator input. Darkness responds to measured drive motor torque and a short positive-throttle transient. A soot intensity slider (0–6x, default 3x) scales generation and darkness; zero stops new emissions and existing particles fade out. Particles inherit tailpipe point velocity, receive a rearward jet, then disperse with drag and buoyancy. Opacity fades with age. Local soot staining accumulates slowly around the tailpipe and resets with the run. Brake torque is excluded from load. The pool is capped at 420 and particle life at 7.3 seconds. At saturation the oldest puff is retired to keep fresh emission continuous. Spatial eddies, velocity relaxation, randomized lobes and a rising flow disperse the jet; clouds remain in world space after throttle release. Air clipping keeps the cloud above the actual ground segments. Pause freezes simulation and particles. No runtime files or logs accumulate.

Rendering correction: the rubber contour and steel rim share the physical hub center and tire angular position. The loaded rubber contour follows the exact piecewise-linear collision surface; the steel rim stays circular. Contact detection controls sidewall widening, and the terrain constraint also applies on the frame before a new contact begins. Airborne wheels away from the surface are round. The road stroke is clipped below the surface instead of covering the contact patch. This does not change the rigid circular collision shape or introduce a deformable carcass.

## Compliance and wheel-tub correction (current)

The prior revision engaged a 12000 N cubic buffer only 10 mm beyond the target ride position. Together with 3.2 Hz default springs and painted-lip collision geometry, this restricted free movement and caused abrupt tire-to-liner impulses. Defaults are now 1.7 Hz / 0.50 damping; Comfort 1.3 / 0.40 and Sport 2.6 / 0.65. The +0.14 m bump engagement leaves 95 mm beyond static target before progressively rising toward the +0.24 m stop, with a 6500 N cubic coefficient over 0.10 m. Chassis reference height is 1.22 m.

Visible arch lips (ellipse radii 0.55 / 0.61 m, center y=-0.62 m) and inner wheel tubs (envelope radii 0.65 / 0.92 m) are distinct. A compressed tire may be occluded by the painted fender while remaining inside the physical wheel tub. Tests now measure physical clearance against that tub, rather than incorrectly forbidding realistic visual occlusion. Ground clearance and travel-limit assertions remain. The physical liner is 0.04 m inside the tub envelope.

Exhaust mounts integrate a 3.6 Hz damped hanger displacement driven by local chassis acceleration and engine pulses, with 45 mm bounded movement. Muffler, rubber hanger and tailpipe are drawn from that state; emitted particles use the same moving tip and velocity. This is a simplified hanger model, not a finite-element exhaust assembly.

## Worker execution and rendering optimization (2026-11-04)

The same Planck model now executes in a dedicated browser Web Worker. `physics-host.js` owns simulation creation, tuning and stepping; `simulation-client.js` presents read-only render snapshots to the UI. Snapshot contact nodes are copied directly from the mechanical bodies, with the same outer-normal outline calculation. There is no renderer-only tire replacement or ground projection. The default 120 Hz step, eight substeps, solver iterations, tire pressure/links, suspension constants and rim-driven traction are unchanged. The terrain height query now reuses precomputed collision-grid samples instead of recomputing trigonometric terrain heights for every smoke particle.

Only one worker step job may be in flight. At most six fixed steps (50 ms) are retained for catch-up, and every new job receives current controls/settings. Reset generations discard stale replies. Pause/blur clear input and retained time; workers have no autonomous timer that could continue driving a hidden tab. A severely overloaded device slows simulated time instead of building an unlimited queue. Each 120 Hz state is retained in the reply, including intermediate states within a batch. The renderer interpolates the body, wheels, actual contact nodes and exhaust mount on one shared timeline with a nominal 50 ms buffer. It never extrapolates beyond a solved state. Buffer recovery changes playback rate by at most +3% / -5%; packet arrival alone cannot move the displayed pose. This moves CPU work off the UI thread rather than reducing the solver's computational cost.

New regressions compare an actual worker's complete snapshots against direct Planck execution through AWD/FWD/RWD, pressure/tuning changes and braking. They also check bounded queued work, reset generation handling, live node access and surfaced worker failure. Existing physical tests remain mandatory.

Smoke draws from 51 precomputed 128 x 128 sprites (about 3.2 MiB RGBA) with per-particle scale, rotation, darkness and fading. Soft billows accumulate on a half-CSS-resolution layer, then composite once through the full-resolution ground clip. This cuts particle fill area by 4x at DPR 1 or 16x at DPR 2; these are pixel-count ratios, not measured speedups. Car, terrain and panorama keep their display resolution. The bounded 420-particle world-space lifecycle and load/throttle response remain. Tonal quantization and precomposited lobe opacity are artistic approximations, not a fluid model. Offscreen billows are culled; the road query visits only its visible ordered grid slice. HUD writes are limited to 10 Hz. Paused/finished canvases redraw only when invalidated by a UI toggle, resize or image load. The optional `?profile` diagnostics retain at most 300 frame samples in memory and expose buffer occupancy, render-clock holds and draw count; no runtime traces or telemetry are written.

The initial worker revision displayed only the last received state immediately and smoothed the camera independently. This caused holds followed by jumps when replies arrived unevenly, despite low JavaScript callback times. Current horizontal camera position follows the same interpolated chassis x coordinate exactly, keeping the vehicle's reference point fixed on screen. Vertical follow uses exponential smoothing. Contact-node interpolation is presentation between physical states; it does not replace pneumatic deformation or feed forces back into Planck. The short playback buffer introduces about 50 ms of presentation latency, plus scheduling delay. Severe worker/compositor stalls can still produce a held frame; a local timing sample does not prove universal smoothness.

Implementation reference: [MDN, Using Web Workers](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Workers_API/Using_web_workers). The server remains a loopback-only static file service; it is not the gameplay solver. It now supports revalidation with ETags, HEAD, current-file invalidation after rebuild, and a maximum 16-file/16-MiB memory cache.

## 2026-11-21 · Larger road wheels and an 800 m course

The game now uses a 0.520 m unloaded tire radius and a 0.275 m rigid rim radius (previously 0.480 / 0.245 m). Both axles spawn 40 mm higher. The liner, painted openings, rolling-speed conversion and wheel-face rendering respect the larger assembly. The contact ring, pressure-volume model and fixed solver step are the same. The wheel and suspension tests exercise the new dimensions; earlier bench values above describe their dated builds.

Stock axle torque is 1850 N·m, motor launch allowance 4.3 rad/s, and stock gear limit 22 rad/s. These are wheel-drive model parameters. Body levels 5–8 add progressively stronger game aero: downward force rises with the square of longitudinal speed and body angular damping rises from 0.6 to 1.55. Downforce enters the sprung body during each substep. Driving remains a consequence of ground contact. The longer hill entrance blends out short ripples and spreads the climb over 40 m.

## Wet asphalt and mud

The contact edge material sets friction to 0.95 on dry asphalt, 0.68 under standing water and 0.40 on muddy beds. The solver applies opposing viscous forces to submerged tread nodes and adds longitudinal ploughing resistance at the axle proportional to immersion. Tire motion in contact with the layer drives spray, mud deposition and washing. The free surface stays level over a fixed roadbed. The model does not simulate rut formation, bulk fluid displacement or hydroplaning.

## 2026-11-21 · Crankshaft rating, clutch and measured wheel power

The eight tuning stages now specify torque at rated crank speed. Catalog power in kW is torque in N·m times angular speed in rad/s, divided by 1000. The stock engine is 60 N·m at 3500 rpm (29.9 metric hp); the top engine is 200 N·m at 6100 rpm (173.7 hp before the compressor). These are gameplay engines, without a measured dynamometer curve.

The drive model estimates crank speed from the powered hubs, four stepped ratios, and an idle/launch speed floor for clutch slip. Available wheel torque is constrained by the engine torque curve, 86% driveline efficiency, the fitted axle torque limit and the wheel-speed governor. The stock clutch and driveline transmit at most 95 N·m at the reference tire radius; the first gearbox tops out near 8 km/h. The pressure-driven tire nodes still deliver traction at the road. Live wheel power integrates torque times angular displacement on the powered joints across all eight substeps. Overrun gives no positive driving output. Force, crankshaft rating, actual axle output and gearing are separate readings.

The tire link uses Planck's soft distance constraint, specialized for a centre-mounted, fixed-rotation contact bead at its B end. The A end can be an offset anchor on a rotating rim. Initialization reuses one direction vector, and the velocity loop updates scalar components in place. It preserves the effective mass, spring softness, warm start and reciprocal linear/angular impulses. This specialization depends on the pinned Planck 1.5.1 solver fields. A varying-timestep regression compares it directly against the generic joint. The eight 960 Hz substeps, iteration counts and all tread colliders remain active.
